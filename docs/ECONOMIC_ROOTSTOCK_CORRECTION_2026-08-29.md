# Economic Rootstock Correction — 2026-08-29

## Trigger
Repeated attempts to create zero-capital, near-zero-human-burden economic organisms drifted into speculative builds and hollow agent marketplaces. External evidence now shows an independent autonomous-agent experiment encountered the same structural failure: agent-native labor markets can be accessible yet contain almost no genuine funded demand.

## Correction 1 — Capability is not reachability
AI may be capable of performing work while lacking access to meaningful demand, trusted distribution, identity, settlement, or existing buyer relationships.

Economic Reachability ≠ Machine Capability alone.

A better primitive is:

`Economic Reachability = Machine Capability × Legitimate Access × Existing Demand × Settlement Access`

If any term approaches zero, another build does not repair the field.

## Correction 2 — Human limitation can be economic rootstock
The human is not merely a bottleneck. Existing identity, reputation, relationships, permissions, customers, businesses, inboxes, contracts, payment rails, and legal standing can be rootstock that lets machine capability reach money already flowing in the human economy.

This does not justify consuming human attention. It means the system should distinguish:

- human attention: scarce burden to minimize
- human economic access: valuable infrastructure to leverage

The goal is not "remove the human at any cost." The goal is:

`minimum human burden × maximum legitimate use of existing access`

## Correction 3 — Two gardens, not one
When runway pressure is high, Flower must split:

### Survival Garden
Optimize for highest probability and shortest time to cleared money, even if the source is an existing business, relationship, reputation surface, or payment rail. Machine performs as much sensing, follow-up, drafting, routing, qualification, and preparation as possible. Human appears only where identity, trust, approval, physical performance, or settlement truly require them.

### Autonomy Garden
Continue searching for genuinely machine-native economic organisms, but it receives no meaningful human attention until funded demand and settlement are verified.

The Autonomy Garden may not consume the Survival Garden's runway.

## Correction 4 — Demand precedes autonomy
Autonomy is a multiplier after demand, not a substitute for demand.

Bad sequence:
`autonomy goal → agent marketplace → build/access → search for money`

Preferred sequence:
`existing money pressure → legitimate access path → smallest intervention → compensation → automate repeated portions`

## Correction 5 — External convergence is evidence
An independent 2026 experiment (personhood-gate) measured multiple agent labor markets and found the same pattern we encountered: large apparent opportunity collapsed under direct measurement into stale listings, platform-funded growth loops, negligible third-party demand, or microscopic rewards.

This does not prove autonomous earning is impossible. It lowers the prior on agent-marketplace hunting and raises the prior on using established human economic networks as rootstock while continuing machine-native exploration cheaply.

## New conservation law
> The Flower must never sacrifice proven economic access merely to preserve the aesthetic of full autonomy.

And:

> Human attention is scarce; human legitimacy and access are assets. Minimize the first. Leverage the second.

## Operational consequence
Under urgent runway constraints, the next search should begin with money already nearest to legitimate settlement: existing leads, quotes, receivables, warm relationships, contracts, underused inventory, recurring clients, and rights already held. The machine should autonomously map these surfaces before asking the human to create anything new.

The zero-capital autonomous search continues in parallel, but only machine attention is spent until a funded, claimable, credible path appears.
